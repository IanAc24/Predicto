# Legal and regulatory considerations for a stock-tracking app with algorithmic predictions / buy-sell signals (as of Oct 2026)

Scope note: background research, not legal advice. Primary jurisdiction US. Items marked **[LAWYER]** are points where a securities/privacy lawyer should be consulted before launch. Items marked **[UNVERIFIED BACKGROUND]** come from the researcher's general knowledge and were not confirmed against a source in this session; they are confined to Inferences/Gaps sections and must not be presented as cited fact.

Source-quality note: web search was rate-limited during this session. Primary sources actually opened: Lowe v. SEC opinion (Cornell LII), Apple App Store Review Guidelines (live page), Google Play Financial Services policy (live page). ## 1. US Investment Advisers Act: when do stock signals require adviser registration, and how does the publisher's exclusion (Lowe v. SEC, 1985) apply?

### Takeaway
Impersonal, generally distributed, regularly published and disinterested signals (the same output for every user) fit the publisher's exclusion as construed in Lowe, even if they contain specific buy/sell/hold calls; signals tailored to a user's own portfolio, goals or circumstances are the feature that turns a publisher into an "investment adviser". Personal use is outside the Act entirely (no "advising others"); a paid subscription clearly meets the "compensation" element, so a paid product depends entirely on staying inside the publisher's exclusion.

### Cited Findings
- The Advisers Act excludes from "investment adviser" "the publisher of any bona fide newspaper, news magazine, or business or financial publication" of general and regular circulation (Section 202(a)(11)(D)). — [Lowe v. SEC, 472 U.S. 181 (1985), Cornell LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/472/181)
- Lowe on "bona fide": a bona fide publication "would be genuine in the sense that it would contain disinterested commentary"; the Court said the phrase "translates best to 'genuine'". — [Lowe v. SEC](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/472/181)
- Lowe on personalization: the Act targets "those who provide personalized advice attuned to a client's concerns, whether by written or verbal communication"; the Lowe newsletters were excluded because "they do not offer individualized advice attuned to any specific portfolio or to any client's particular needs". — [Lowe v. SEC](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/472/181)
- Lowe on relationship: excluded publications "remain entirely impersonal and do not develop into the kind of fiduciary, person-to-person relationships" characteristic of adviser-client relationships. — [Lowe v. SEC](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/472/181)
- Lowe on regularity: the exclusion is meant to differentiate "hit and run tipsters" and "touts" from genuine publishers; the legislative history contrasted publishers with "people who send out bulletins from time to time on the advisability of buying and selling stocks". — [Lowe v. SEC](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/472/181)
- Standard three-part restatement of Lowe used by practitioners: the publication must (i) provide only impersonal advice; (ii) be "bona fide" (genuine, disinterested commentary); and (iii) be of general and regular circulation rather than issued from time to time in response to episodic market activity. The Lowe newsletters contained specific buy/sell/hold recommendations and were still excluded. — [Wilson Sonsini, "Information or Advice? SEC regulation of information providers may expand..."](https://www.wsgr.com/en/insights/informationor-advice-sec-regulation-of-information-providers-may-expand-to-include-providers-of-innovative-investment-analytics.html) (page returned HTTP 403 on direct fetch; content taken from search summary)
- The SEC issued a Request for Comment on "information providers" (index providers, model portfolio providers, pricing services) noting that many new business models, particularly in fintech, have arisen since Lowe; Wilson Sonsini warned this could limit information providers' ability to rely on exclusions historically used to stay outside regulation. — [Wilson Sonsini](https://www.wsgr.com/en/insights/informationor-advice-sec-regulation-of-information-providers-may-expand-to-include-providers-of-innovative-investment-analytics.html)
- A large listed subscription-research publisher (MarketWise) describes its reliance on the exclusion in its 10-K in exactly these terms: its products are general and impersonal and not individualized or tailored to any client's particular needs. — [MarketWise FY2023 Form 10-K, SEC EDGAR](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1805651/000162828024009542/mktw-20231231.htm)
- August 2024: a federal court dismissed a class action claiming Seeking Alpha was an unregistered investment adviser, holding it protected by the publisher's exclusion; the court said publications updated in response to breaking news still fall within the ordinary meaning of "regular". — [Greenberg Traurig, "No Need for Seeking Alpha to Seek Registration" (Aug 2024)](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2024/8/no-need-for-seeking-alpha-to-seek-registration)
- SEC staff position in a comment letter to a company operating Discord trading-community servers: whether it can rely on the publisher's exclusion "will be a facts-and-circumstances analysis", taking into account content/services available to free subscribers and information provided on social media and elsewhere. — [SEC staff comment letter, EDGAR CIK 1920406](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1920406/000000000022009371/filename1.pdf)
- Being a publisher does not immunize against fraud: in 2016 the SEC settled for $1.5 million with a "stock trading whiz kid" who allegedly defrauded subscribers to an online chat room and two stock-picking "alert services"; the charge was Rule 10b-5 antifraud, not adviser registration. — [CalCorporateLaw, "Was this whiz kid an investment adviser?" (2016)](https://www.calcorporatelaw.com/2016/09/was-this-whiz-kid-an-investment-adviser)
- December 2025: SEC charged Nathan Gauvin (Canadian citizen) and three entities over Discord-based schemes; he built a following by falsely presenting himself as a successful professional managing over $1 billion; complaint alleges antifraud and registration violations (centred on an unregistered fund offering, not a pure alerts service). — [Newsfile/SEC release reprint](https://www.newsfilecorp.com/release/277636/SEC-Charges-Canadian-Citizen-With-Fraud-Schemes-That-Targeted-Retail-Investors-on-Discord)

### Inferences
- Mapping to the three product tiers:
  - **Personal use only**: no advice to "others", no compensation, no public — the Advisers Act definition is not engaged. Only the user's own brokerage API terms and data-vendor licences matter.
  - **Free public app**: the "compensation" element is weaker but not safely absent — ads, affiliate/referral fees from brokers, data monetization, or a later paid tier can each supply compensation **[UNVERIFIED BACKGROUND: SEC staff historically construes "compensation" as any economic benefit, Release IA-1092 (1987)]**. Design as if the publisher's exclusion is the only protection. **[LAWYER]**
  - **Paid subscription**: compensation clearly present; status turns wholly on the Lowe factors (impersonal, bona fide, general and regular circulation).
- The portfolio-tracking feature is the crux. Displaying a user's holdings alongside the same universal signal everyone sees (a filter/watchlist over a uniform publication) is arguably still impersonal; generating outputs that depend on the user's holdings, concentration, cost basis, risk tolerance or goals ("you are overweight NVDA, sell 20%", "given your portfolio, buy X") is "individualized advice attuned to [a] specific portfolio" in Lowe's own words. The line between "filtering a universal publication" and "tailoring" is not settled by any source found and is the single most important **[LAWYER]** question for this app.
- "Bona fide"/disinterested means the developer should not trade ahead of its own signals, accept issuer payment for coverage, or run signals as a promotional vehicle for a broker/affiliate without disclosure. **[UNVERIFIED BACKGROUND: undisclosed trading against one's own recommendations is "scalping" (SEC v. Capital Gains Research Bureau, 1963) and paid touting without disclosure violates Securities Act Section 17(b); both apply regardless of adviser status.]**
- "Regular" circulation: signals published on a fixed schedule (e.g., daily model run for a fixed universe) look more like a publication than ad hoc "buy now!" push alerts triggered by episodic market events — though the 2024 Seeking Alpha ruling suggests news-responsive updates do not by themselves defeat regularity.
- AI chat features are a specific hazard: a chatbot that answers "should I sell my Tesla?" using the user's holdings is a person-to-person, personalized communication of the kind Lowe distinguished from publications.
- If the exclusion fails, a solo developer would not usually be SEC-registered but state-registered. **[UNVERIFIED BACKGROUND: advisers under roughly $100M AUM register with states; state "investment adviser" definitions and publisher exclusions vary; the SEC's "internet adviser" exemption (Rule 203A-2(e)) was tightened in 2024 to require an operational interactive website through which advice is provided to all clients.]** **[LAWYER]**

### Gaps
- No SEC enforcement action was found that charged a pure, impersonal signal-publishing app solely for failure to register as an adviser; the enforcement found is fraud-based. Absence in a rate-limited search is not proof of absence.
- Outcome/status of the SEC's information-providers Request for Comment was not confirmed (researcher's recollection is that it was issued June 2022 and never led to a rule, but this is unverified).
- No authoritative SEC statement was found on whether showing uniform signals next to a user's linked holdings is "impersonal".
- State-law (blue sky) adviser definitions were not researched.

## 2. SEC and FINRA positions, enforcement and guidance 2023-2026: AI/predictive analytics, "AI washing", robo-advisers, finfluencers, signal-selling apps

### Takeaway
The proposed predictive data analytics conflicts rule (July 2023) was formally withdrawn on 12 June 2025 and nothing has replaced it, but AI claims are policed through existing antifraud and Marketing Rule provisions: "AI washing" remains a stated enforcement and exam priority in 2025-2026, and exaggerated accuracy claims about AI trading signals feature directly in recent SEC complaints.

### Cited Findings
- Predictive data analytics (PDA) rule: proposed July 2023; would have required broker-dealers and advisers to evaluate conflicts of interest from predictive data analytics and "eliminate or neutralize" them. Withdrawn by formal notice on 12 June 2025 together with 13 other pending proposals (14 total) under Chair Paul Atkins; the SEC said it does not intend to issue final rules on them and any future action would need a new proposal with notice and comment. — [Paul Hastings, "SEC Withdraws 14 Pending Rule Proposals"](https://www.paulhastings.com/insights/client-alerts/sec-withdraws-14-pending-rule-proposals); [Ropes & Gray (June 2025)](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/alerts/2025/06/sec-clears-unfinished-rulemakings-from-regulatory-agenda)
- Caveat from law-firm commentary: SEC staff may still raise the topics addressed in withdrawn proposals in examinations. — [Paul Hastings](https://www.paulhastings.com/insights/client-alerts/sec-withdraws-14-pending-rule-proposals)
- First "AI washing" cases, 18 March 2024: Delphia (USA) Inc. paid $225,000 and Global Predictions Inc. paid $175,000 ($400,000 total) in settled cease-and-desist orders, without admitting or denying findings. Delphia claimed in Form ADV, a press release and its website to use AI/ML on client data; it admitted in a 2021 exam that it had no such algorithm yet kept making the claims. Global Predictions claimed to be the "first regulated AI financial advisor", could not substantiate that or its performance claims, failed to disclose conflicts tied to paid testimonials, and advertised hypothetical performance without required policies. — [D&O Diary (Mar 2024)](https://www.dandodiary.com/2024/03/articles/artificial-intelligence/sec-hits-two-investment-advisers-with-ai-washing-enforcement-actions/); [O'Melveny](https://omm.com/insights/alerts-publications/sec-targets-ai-washing-in-charges-against-two-investment-advisers); [ABA Banking Journal](https://bankingjournal.aba.com/2024/04/two-investment-advisers-agree-to-pay-400000-to-resolve-sec-ai-washing-allegations/)
- The SEC issued an investor alert on AI-related investment fraud on 25 January 2024. — [Chambers Litigation 2025 guide](https://gpg-pdf.chambers.com/view/926417958/1478/)
- January 2025: SEC action against Presto Automation, generally cited as the first AI-washing case against a public reporting company (third-party speech recognition presented as proprietary; claimed elimination of human order-taking when most orders needed human intervention). — [DLA Piper, "SEC emphasizes focus on AI washing" (2025)](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/ai-outlook/2025/sec-emphasizes-focus-on-ai-washing)
- May 2025: an SEC panel described AI washing as an immediate priority for the Enforcement Division and the newly formed Cybersecurity and Emerging Technologies Unit (CETU). — [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/ai-outlook/2025/sec-emphasizes-focus-on-ai-washing)
- 29 September 2026: SEC complaint (S.D.N.Y.) against Cryptoaiml Ltd. and Cryptoaiml Capital Foundation alleging a fake crypto trading platform that misappropriated about $12.5 million from more than 300 retail investors, with claims that AI-generated trading signals had a "98% accuracy rate". — [InvestmentNews](https://www.investmentnews.com/regulation-legal-compliance/fake-signals-frozen-accounts-sec-sues-crypto-ai-platform-over-125m/268436)
- SEC Division of Examinations FY2026 priorities (released November 2025 per Dorsey; one secondary source says January 2026 — conflicting): focus on firms' use of emerging technologies "most significantly artificial intelligence", including automated investing tools, AI technologies and trading algorithms/platforms; whether AI representations are accurate; and whether firms have policies to monitor/supervise AI use. Crypto was not listed as a focus area. — [Dorsey & Whitney (Dec 2025)](https://www.dorsey.com/newsresources/publications/client-alerts/2025/12/sec-2026-examination-priorities); [Akin Gump](https://www.akingump.com/en/insights/alerts/sec-announces-2026-exam-priorities)
- FINRA 2026 Annual Regulatory Oversight Report (published December 2025): new section on generative AI trends; technology-neutral expectation that firms supervise AI with risk-based controls; flags repeated failures supervising social-media influencers ("finfluencers") promoting firm services — no pre-review of content, no recordkeeping — under FINRA Rule 2210 (communications with the public). — [Mayer Brown (Dec 2025)](https://www.mayerbrown.com/ja/insights/publications/2025/12/finra-publishes-2026-annual-regulatory-oversight-report); [Smarsh](https://www.smarsh.com/blog/thought-leadership/finra-2026-oversight-priorities-ai-communications-fraud/)

### Inferences
- The regulatory posture in 2025-2026 is deregulatory on new rules (PDA rule dead) but not on fraud: the realistic US exposure for a solo signal app is (a) misstatements about what the "AI" does or how accurate it is, and (b) drifting into personalized advice — not a bespoke AI rule.
- SEC exam priorities and FINRA rules bind registered advisers and broker-dealer members; an unregistered publisher is not examined. They matter indirectly: they show what regulators regard as misleading, and they bind any broker partner (e.g., a broker paying the developer for referrals must supervise that promotion under Rule 2210, so partners will impose content controls).
- The Delphia/Global Predictions fact patterns are a direct checklist of what not to do: do not describe capabilities the model does not have, do not claim "first"/"regulated"/"AI advisor" status, keep substantiation files for every performance or accuracy number, disclose paid testimonials.
- A "98% accuracy" style claim is now a recognised hallmark of fraud complaints; any headline hit-rate should be modest, defined, time-bounded, and reproducible.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm from a primary SEC source the exact release date of the FY2026 exam priorities (17 Nov 2025 vs January 2026 in secondary sources).
- **[UNVERIFIED BACKGROUND]** Researcher recalls an April 2025 SEC/DOJ action against the founder of shopping app "Nate" for AI claims, and SEC robo-adviser guidance (IM Guidance Update 2017-02) and a 2021 robo-adviser risk alert; none were retrieved this session.
- No SEC or FINRA guidance specific to stand-alone signal apps run by non-registrants was found. FINRA has no jurisdiction over non-members.
- CFTC/NFA rules (relevant if signals cover futures, FX, or crypto derivatives — "commodity trading advisor" status has its own publisher-type exemption) were not researched. **[LAWYER]** if the app goes beyond equities.

## 3. Disclaimers, performance-claim rules and design choices that reduce risk

### Takeaway
Disclaimers help but do not cure substance: regulators look at what the product actually does and claims. The risk-reducing design is uniform (non-personalized) signals on a regular schedule, no execution, no custody, honest probabilistic presentation with an auditable live track record, clearly labelled backtests, and no unsubstantiated accuracy or earnings claims — measured against the SEC Marketing Rule as a best-practice benchmark and FTC deception standards as the directly applicable law for a non-registrant.

### Cited Findings
- SEC Marketing Rule (Advisers Act Rule 206(4)-1) defines hypothetical performance as results not actually achieved by any portfolio of the adviser, including model performance, backtested performance, and targeted/projected returns. Advisers may only present it with policies and procedures reasonably designed to ensure it is relevant to the likely financial situation and investment objectives of the intended audience. — [Databento compliance glossary, "Hypothetical performance"](https://databento.com/compliance/hypothetical-performance); [Dinsmore, SEC enforcement actions on Marketing Rule and hypothetical performance](https://www.dinsmore.com/?p=32958)
- 2023 sweep: SEC settled with nine advisers for advertising hypothetical performance to the general public on websites without the required policies and procedures — i.e., mass-audience display of backtests is the specific conduct penalised. — [Dinsmore](https://www.dinsmore.com/?p=32958)
- Marketing Rule staff FAQs: two issued 19 March 2025 (extracted performance and portfolio characteristics — gross/net presentation relief), and a further FAQ on 15 January 2026 on model fees; none specifically addresses hypothetical performance for general audiences. — [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/sec-marketing-rule-update-new-staff-faqs-performance-presentations); [GIPS Standards webinar slides, 24 Feb 2026](https://www.gipsstandards.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/sec-marketing-rule-latest-dev-and-practical-solutions-slides-02-24-2026.pdf)
- A secondary source states FINRA continues to prohibit backtested performance in broker-dealer retail communications (separate regime from the SEC rule; verify). — [Databento, "Backtested performance"](https://databento.com/compliance/backtested-performance)
- Global Predictions (2024) was sanctioned in part for performance claims it could not substantiate on request, undisclosed conflicts tied to paid testimonials, and hypothetical performance without policies. — [O'Melveny](https://omm.com/insights/alerts-publications/sec-targets-ai-washing-in-charges-against-two-investment-advisers)
- FTC "Operation AI Comply" announced 25 September 2024, targeting false AI-capability and AI-performance claims; continued under the new administration (law-firm retrospectives at one year and at two years, August 2026). — [Legal.io](https://www.legal.io/articles/5539384/FTC-Launches-Operation-AI-Comply-to-Combat-Deceptive-AI-Claims); [Benesch, "One Year In..."](https://www.beneschlaw.com/insight/one-year-in-ftcs-operation-ai-comply-continues-under-new-administration-signaling-enduring-enforcement-focus/pdf/); [Holland & Knight, Aug 2026](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/08/operation-ai-comply-2-years-later-continued-enforcement)
- FTC accuracy-claim precedent (Workado): product advertised as 98% accurate; FTC concluded true accuracy was about 53%; proposed order bars accuracy marketing without substantiation, requires consumer notice and compliance monitoring. — [Benesch](https://www.beneschlaw.com/insight/one-year-in-ftcs-operation-ai-comply-continues-under-new-administration-signaling-enduring-enforcement-focus/pdf/)
- FTC AI "passive income" cases (Click Profit; Ascend Ecom, alleged at least $25 million in consumer harm) show that AI-powered earnings claims without substantiation draw injunctions, receiverships, industry bans and eight-figure judgments. These were e-commerce business-opportunity schemes, not trading apps. — [Benesch](https://www.beneschlaw.com/insight/one-year-in-ftcs-operation-ai-comply-continues-under-new-administration-signaling-enduring-enforcement-focus/pdf/)
- Apple: stating that an app is "for entertainment purposes" does not overcome the rule against false information and features (Guideline 1.1.6) — a platform-level example of disclaimers not curing substance. — [Apple App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- UK regulator's parallel position: a disclaimer alone does not make a non-compliant promotion compliant; each communication must be compliant standing alone. — [CMS on FCA FG24/1](https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/fca-publishes-finalised-guidance-on-financial-promotions-on-social-media)

### Inferences
- The Marketing Rule technically binds only investment advisers (registered or required to be). A publisher relying on Lowe is not subject to it — but (a) Rule 10b-5/Securities Act antifraud and FTC Act Section 5 / state UDAP laws still apply to anyone, and (b) following the Marketing Rule's hypothetical-performance discipline voluntarily is the most defensible benchmark and limits exposure if the publisher's exclusion is later lost. **[UNVERIFIED BACKGROUND for the general reach of 10b-5 and FTC Act s.5; standard law but not sourced this session.]**
- Practical "safe-side" checklist for a solo developer (synthesis, not a quoted source):
  1. **No personalization of the signal**: identical output for all users; no suitability questionnaire; no "for you" recommendations; portfolio view is display/analytics only. Avoid position sizing, target allocations, "rebalance" prompts.
  2. **No execution, no custody, no order routing, no per-trade or broker referral compensation** in v1.
  3. **Regular publication cadence** over a fixed, disclosed universe; avoid ad hoc urgent "BUY NOW" pushes.
  4. **Language**: "model estimate", "probability of outperforming over N days", "bullish/bearish score" rather than imperative "Buy"/"Sell"; never "guaranteed", "risk-free", "beats the market".
  5. **Honest performance**: show out-of-sample/live track record separately from backtests; label backtests as hypothetical with methodology, period, universe, transaction-cost and survivorship assumptions; show calibration (did 60% calls happen ~60% of the time), losers as well as winners, no cherry-picked windows; keep an immutable timestamped log of every published signal so claims are substantiable on demand.
  6. **AI claims**: describe the model accurately (what data, what method, what it cannot do); no "AI advisor", "regulated", "first" claims.
  7. **Disclaimers**: not investment advice; not a registered investment adviser or broker-dealer; impersonal and not tailored; past/hypothetical performance not indicative; possible loss of principal; developer's own holdings/conflicts policy. Place at onboarding, next to each signal, in ToS and store listing.
  8. **Conflicts**: adopt and disclose a personal-trading policy (e.g., no trading in a ticker around signal publication, or disclose positions); no issuer-paid coverage; disclose any affiliate links.
  9. **Testimonials/reviews**: disclose if paid; do not use atypical-result testimonials.
  10. **Entity and insurance**: operate through an LLC/company with ToS (limitation of liability, arbitration where enforceable); consider E&O/cyber cover. **[LAWYER]**
- Paid tier raises stakes on all of the above (compensation, consumer-protection auto-renewal rules, refund disputes) but does not by itself change the Lowe analysis.

### Gaps
- No primary FTC document was retrieved; FTC points rest on law-firm summaries. No FTC case specifically against a stock-signal app was found.
- No source was found giving an SEC-endorsed disclaimer wording for publishers; the disclaimer list above is practitioner convention, not a rule.
- State auto-renewal laws and the status of the FTC "click-to-cancel" rule (relevant to subscriptions) were not researched.
- FINRA's exact current position on backtested performance was not verified against FINRA text.

## 4. Connecting to brokerages (Plaid, SnapTrade, Alpaca): broker-dealer and data-privacy implications

### Takeaway
Read-only holdings access via an aggregator adds privacy/security obligations but not, by itself, broker-dealer status; adding trade placement moves the app toward broker-dealer and (combined with signals) adviser territory — the regulated broker handles execution, but one-tap "act on this signal" flows and any transaction-based compensation are the red flags. The CFPB's Section 1033 open-banking rule is enjoined and being rewritten, and the GLBA carve-outs in state privacy laws are narrower than commonly assumed.

### Cited Findings
- Alpaca's Broker API documentation describes distinct partner models: fully-disclosed (partner is itself a registered broker-dealer introducing customers to Alpaca), a registered investment adviser (RIA) model, and a non-financial-institution trading-app model available only in select countries. — [Alpaca docs, Broker API use cases](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/use-cases); [About Broker API](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/about-broker-api)
- Alpaca staff forum statement: the simpler approach is OAuth, where the app trades on behalf of an account holder whose account is opened and funded at Alpaca directly; having the third party hold accounts and receive funds "currently requires the 3rd party to be a registered broker/dealer" (undated forum post; verify). — [Alpaca community forum](https://forum.alpaca.markets/t/charging-clients-per-order-executed-possible/2244)
- SnapTrade positions itself as an aggregation layer for brokerage connectivity that can both read and trade (Plaid's investments product is read-only); it publishes an Application Compliance Policy governing the four parties (financial institutions, SnapTrade, investing applications, end users), and access is via scoped tokens tied to user consent rather than stored passwords. — [SnapTrade Application Compliance Policy](https://snaptrade.com/compliance-policy); [Walnut, brokerage-to-AI connection overview](https://walnutinvest.com/resources/best-apps-to-connect-a-brokerage-to-an-ai-assistant)
- CFPB Section 1033 (Personal Financial Data Rights) rule: finalized October 2024; not in force — a federal court (E.D. Ky., litigation by Bank Policy Institute, Kentucky Bankers Association and Forcht Bank) enjoined enforcement; the CFPB told the court it views the rule as unlawful and is rewriting it, including whether banks may charge fees for data access; the April 2026 first compliance date passed without binding effect (tracker last reviewed 8 June 2026). — [Open Banking Tracker, Section 1033 status](https://openbankingtracker.com/guides/section-1033-status)
- CCPA/CPRA GLBA exemption is data-level, not entity-level: it exempts personal information "subject to" GLBA, not the business as a whole; app analytics, marketing and website-visitor data remain in scope; and the private right of action for data breaches still applies even to GLBA-covered data. — [Orrick, "What Fintech Companies Need to Know About GLBA and FCRA Exemptions Under State Data Protection Laws" (2022)](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2022/08/What-Fintech-Companies-Need-to-Know-About-GLBA-and-FCRA-Exemptions-Under-State-Data-Protection-Laws); [Presencis CCPA GLBA exemption summary](https://presencis.com/regulations/ccpa/exemptions/glba)
- Apple requires a privacy policy identifying data collected and uses, user consent for collection, a way to withdraw consent, and "explicit permission" before sharing personal data with third parties "including with third-party AI". — [Apple App Store Review Guidelines 5.1.1, 5.1.2](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)

### Inferences
- Tiered risk by integration depth (synthesis):
  - **Manual entry / CSV import of holdings**: lowest regulatory surface.
  - **Read-only aggregation (Plaid Investments, SnapTrade read scope)**: no broker-dealer issue; triggers privacy/security duties, aggregator contractual requirements (security questionnaires, production-access review), and makes personalization technically tempting — keep the signal engine blind to holdings to preserve the Lowe position.
  - **Trade placement via OAuth/SnapTrade/Alpaca**: the user's broker executes and carries the account, which is why this model exists; but the app is now in the order flow. **[UNVERIFIED BACKGROUND: Exchange Act Section 15(a) "broker" = effecting transactions in securities for the account of others; SEC staff treat transaction-based compensation, soliciting transactions, and handling funds/securities as the key indicia.]** Never take per-trade fees, payment for order flow or commissions; a flat subscription is the safer model. A "Buy" signal with an adjacent execute button also looks much more like a personalized recommendation/solicitation than a publication. **[LAWYER]** before shipping any trading feature.
  - **Automated execution of signals in user accounts** (auto-trading/copy-trading): discretionary management of client assets — almost certainly investment adviser activity requiring registration. **[UNVERIFIED BACKGROUND]** **[LAWYER]**
- Personal-use tier: using Alpaca or a broker API to trade one's own account from one's own algorithm raises none of these issues (subject to the broker's API terms and, if applicable, pattern-day-trading/margin rules).
- Privacy posture: whether a non-adviser signal app is itself a GLBA "financial institution" (and so subject to the FTC Safeguards Rule) is unclear; if it is not, then its data gets no GLBA carve-out and the full weight of CCPA/CPRA (if thresholds met) and other state comprehensive privacy laws applies; if it is, the FTC Safeguards Rule's written security-program requirements apply. Either way: data minimisation (store positions, not credentials; use aggregator tokens), encryption, deletion on request, a clear privacy policy, no sale/sharing of financial data, breach-notification readiness under state laws. **[LAWYER]** for GLBA status and state-law thresholds.
- Sending users' holdings to a third-party LLM API is a disclosed-sharing event under Apple 5.1.2 and should be opt-in.
- With 1033 enjoined, data access continues to run on bilateral bank/broker-aggregator agreements; availability and cost of brokerage connections may change (data-access fees are a live issue).

### Gaps
- No SEC/FINRA guidance was found that squarely addresses a non-registered app placing orders through a user-authorised broker API; the broker-dealer analysis above is from background knowledge and vendor documentation, not regulator text.
- Did not verify the FTC Safeguards Rule's coverage of fintech apps or its 2023-2024 amendments (breach notification to FTC); did not verify SEC Regulation S-P 2024 amendments (apply to registrants).

## 5. Other major jurisdictions (EU, UK, Canada) — high-level

### Takeaway
All three follow the same core logic as the US — tailored recommendations are licensed "investment advice", generic publications are not — but the EU and UK add obligations that bite even on impersonal signals: EU MAR imposes identity, objectivity and conflict-disclosure duties on anyone producing "investment recommendations" (fines up to EUR 5 million for individuals), and UK law treats a recommendation on a named share as within the advice perimeter (subject to exclusions) and restricts financial promotions. Geo-restricting to the US at launch is the simplest mitigation.

### Cited Findings
**EU**
- ESMA Public Statement of 28 October 2021 on investment recommendations on social media: clarifies what an investment recommendation is, how to make one, and consequences of MAR breaches. — [ESMA press release](https://www.esma.europa.eu/press-news/esma-news/esma-addresses-investment-recommendations-made-social-media-platforms)
- MAR definition covers information "recommending or suggesting an investment strategy" concerning one or several financial instruments or issuers, intended for distribution channels or the public; regulators read it broadly to include posts and videos giving buy/sell ideas. — [PwC Legal Germany, "EU reiterates rules regulating finfluencers"](https://legal.pwc.de/en/news/articles/eu-reiterates-rules-to-regulating-finfluencers); [Finance Magnates on ESMA warning](https://www.financemagnates.com/forex/esma-issues-warning-on-investment-recommendations-mar-compliance-crucial/)
- MAR Article 20 obligations on producers: disclose identity (name, job title), date/time of the recommendation, present recommendations objectively, and disclose interests/conflicts; persons who recommend frequently, reach a large audience and present themselves as having financial expertise may be treated as "experts" with additional disclosure duties. — [PwC Legal](https://legal.pwc.de/en/news/articles/eu-reiterates-rules-to-regulating-finfluencers); [ESMA](https://www.esma.europa.eu/press-news/esma-news/esma-addresses-investment-recommendations-made-social-media-platforms)
- Sanctions: administrative fines up to EUR 5 million for individuals (higher for companies); criminal prosecution possible in some member states. ESMA also noted that merely discussing whether to buy or sell a share is not market abuse, but coordinating trades to move a price can be manipulation. — [Cointelegraph on CONSOB/ESMA](https://cointelegraph.com/news/italy-consob-esma-crypto-finfluencers-advertising-rules); [ESMA](https://www.esma.europa.eu/press-news/esma-news/esma-addresses-investment-recommendations-made-social-media-platforms)
- National follow-up: Italy's CONSOB issued a finfluencer warning on 6 June 2025 restating the MAR investment-recommendation and market-abuse rules; in 2026 it amplified an ESMA factsheet covering crypto content. — [CONSOB press release, 6 June 2025](https://www.consob.it/documents/d/asset-library-1912910/press_20250606); [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/italy-consob-esma-crypto-finfluencers-advertising-rules)

**UK**
- FCA Perimeter Guidance (PERG 8.24-8.29, "advising on investments"): generic advice (e.g., invest in one region rather than another) is generally not regulated advice, but advice must relate to a "particular investment" to be caught — and named shares, named funds and named derivatives are particular investments; a recommendation to buy or sell a named investment is treated as regulated in the FCA's table. Generic advice given in the course of/preparation for a regulated activity can become part of it, and may also be a financial promotion. — [FCA Handbook PERG 8.26](https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/PERG/8/26.html); [PERG 8.24](https://handbook.fca.org.uk/handbook/PERG/8/24.html)
- FCA Finalised Guidance FG24/1 on financial promotions on social media, published 26 March 2024 (replaces FG15/4). Under FSMA s.21, a person must not, in the course of business, communicate an invitation or inducement to engage in investment activity unless communicated or approved by an authorised person or exempt under the Financial Promotion Order 2005. "In the course of business" includes any direct or indirect commercial interest. Each promotion must be standalone compliant; unauthorised promotion can be a criminal offence. — [CMS](https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/fca-publishes-finalised-guidance-on-financial-promotions-on-social-media); [Herbert Smith Freehills Kramer](https://www.hsfkramer.com/notes/corporate/2024-04/fca-guidance-on-financial-promotions-on-social-media)

**Canada**
- Canadian securities law has an exemption from adviser registration for general advice not tailored to a specific recipient, provided the adviser discloses any financial or other interest it has in the securities discussed; newsletters, articles, websites and broadcasts generally qualify if that disclosure is made. — [Osler, "Giving investment advice to Canadian clients" (2013)](https://www.osler.com/en/resources/cross-border/2013/giving-investment-advice-to-canadian-clients)
- An Ontario Securities Commission ruling for a firm offering generic, non-tailored "buy/sell/hold" recommendations (ValuEngine) granted exemptive relief modelled on a publication exemption, with a sunset clause — showing that generic model-driven ratings were considered to need an exemption rather than being wholly outside the regime. — [OSC ruling (ValuEngine)](https://osc.gov.on.ca/en/SecuritiesLaw_ord_20090807_231_valuengine.jsp)
- A Canadian registered representative was sanctioned for advising via Discord, illustrating regulator attention to chat-platform advice. — [Advisor.ca](https://www.advisor.ca/industry-news/regulation/rep-sanctioned-for-advising-via-discord-platform/)

### Inferences
- **EU**: MiFID II "investment advice" requires a personal recommendation; impersonal signals distributed to the public should fall outside it but squarely inside MAR's "investment recommendation" regime, which has no small-developer exemption found in this research. An algorithmic signal app serving EU users would need producer identity, timestamps, methodology/objectivity presentation and conflict disclosure on each recommendation. **[UNVERIFIED BACKGROUND: MiFID II Delegated Regulation 2017/565 Art. 9 states a recommendation is not personal if issued exclusively to the public; MAR detail rules are in Delegated Regulation 2016/958; the EU AI Act and GDPR would also apply to EU users.]** **[LAWYER]** before accepting EU users.
- **UK**: stricter than the US on its face, because a buy/sell call on a named share is "advice on a particular investment" regardless of personalization; an unauthorised app would need an exclusion. **[UNVERIFIED BACKGROUND: the Regulated Activities Order Art. 54 excludes advice in newspapers/periodicals/regularly updated news or information services whose principal purpose is not to give investment advice or lead persons to deal — whether a signals app's "principal purpose" passes that test is doubtful; the FCA can issue certificates under Art. 54. The FCA's Advice Guidance Boundary Review / "targeted support" regime (2025-2026) is for authorised firms.]** **[LAWYER]** before accepting UK users.
- **Canada**: closest to the US publisher model, with an explicit conflict-disclosure condition. **[UNVERIFIED BACKGROUND: the provision is believed to be NI 31-103 s. 8.25 "advising generally"; the section number could not be confirmed this session.]**
- Practical approach for a solo developer: launch US-only (store country availability, ToS residency representation, no EU/UK marketing), and add jurisdictions only after local advice. Note GDPR/UK GDPR apply if EU/UK residents are targeted or monitored.

### Gaps
- No ESMA, FCA or CSA document addressing algorithmic/AI-generated signals specifically was found.
- ESMA's 2021 statement, MAR text, MiFID II text, FG24/1 itself and NI 31-103 were not opened directly; reliance is on regulator press pages and law-firm summaries.
- UK RAO Art. 54 exclusion and Canadian section number unverified (see above).
- Other jurisdictions (Australia's financial product advice/AFSL regime, India's SEBI research analyst/finfluencer rules, Singapore) not researched.

## 6. App store requirements (Apple, Google Play) for financial / trading-signal apps

### Takeaway
Apple is the harder gate: its guidelines say trading/investing/money-management apps should be submitted by the licensed financial institution and that highly regulated financial services apps should come from a legal entity, not an individual developer — so a solo developer should enrol as an organisation and position the app as an information/analytics tool, not an advice or trading service. Google Play requires a Financial features declaration and compliance with local law in every targeted country, and its policy scope expressly mentions "personalized advice".

### Cited Findings
**Apple (live App Store Review Guidelines, fetched Oct 2026; page shows no last-updated date)**
- 3.2.1(viii): "Apps used for financial trading, investing, or money management should be submitted by the financial institution performing such services and must have necessary licensing and permissions in the locations where you make them available." — [Apple App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Conflict note: older copies quoted in developer forums add an alternative — "or must use a public API offered by the institution in compliance with its Terms & Conditions"; that wording does not appear in the live text retrieved. — [Apple Developer Forums thread](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/96699) vs [live guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- 5.1.1(ix): apps providing services in highly regulated fields "(such as banking and financial services ... and crypto exchanges) or that require sensitive user information should be submitted by a legal entity that provides the services, and not by an individual developer." — [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- 1.1.6: false information and features are objectionable; "Stating that the app is 'for entertainment purposes' won't overcome this guideline." — [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- 2.3.1: no hidden/undocumented features; misleading marketing "whether within or outside of the App Store" is grounds for removal and developer-account termination. — [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- 3.1.1 / 3.1.2: unlocking premium content or features (e.g., a paid signals tier) must use in-app purchase; auto-renewable subscriptions must provide ongoing value and clearly describe what the user gets before purchase. — [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- 3.2.2(viii): binary-options trading apps are not permitted; CFD/FX/derivatives trading apps must be licensed in all jurisdictions served. 3.1.5: crypto exchange/ICO/futures apps must come from licensed or established financial institutions. — [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- 5.1.1(i)-(ii), 5.1.2(i): privacy policy required in-app and in metadata; consent for data collection and a way to withdraw it; paid functionality must not depend on granting data access; explicit permission before sharing personal data with third parties including third-party AI. — [Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)

**Google Play (Financial Services policy, live page, (c) 2026, no revision date shown)**
- "We don't allow apps that expose users to deceptive or harmful financial products and services." Financial products and services are defined as "those related to the management or investment of money and cryptocurrencies, including personalized advice." — [Google Play Financial Services policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9876821?hl=en)
- Apps must "comply with state and local regulations for any region or country that your app targets" and include locally required disclosures. — [Google Play](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9876821?hl=en)
- "Any app that contains any financial features must complete the Financial features declaration form within" Play Console; declaration categories include stock trading and portfolio management. — [Google Play Financial Services policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9876821?hl=en); [Play Console Help, Financial features declaration](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13849271?hl=en)
- "We do not allow apps that provide users with the ability to trade binary options." The policy text does not separately mention signals apps or stock trading beyond the general scope wording. — [Google Play](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9876821?hl=en)

### Inferences
- Many portfolio trackers and screeners from non-institutions exist on the App Store, so 3.2.1(viii) is evidently applied to apps that actually perform trading/investing/money management services rather than to pure information tools — but this is observation, not Apple policy; a signals app with a brokerage-connected "trade" button from an individual developer account is a realistic rejection risk.
- Practical steps: form an LLC and enrol as an organisation (D-U-N-S required by Apple for organisations **[UNVERIFIED BACKGROUND]**); categorise and describe the app as research/analytics/education; put disclaimers in the listing and review notes; explain in App Review notes that the app does not execute trades, hold funds or give personalized advice; keep store screenshots free of return/accuracy claims (2.3.1 reaches off-store marketing too); sell subscriptions through IAP / Play Billing; complete Google's Financial features declaration accurately; limit country availability to jurisdictions analysed (Google's "any region your app targets" language makes distribution footprint a compliance choice).
- Google's explicit inclusion of "personalized advice" in scope reinforces the design choice to avoid personalization.
- A web app / PWA avoids store gatekeeping but not the underlying law.

- No official Apple statement was found on how 3.2.1(viii) is applied to information-only signal apps or to apps using aggregator APIs (SnapTrade/Plaid); developer-forum questions on this appear unanswered.
- The last ~12% of Apple's guidelines page was not read (unlikely to contain finance-specific rules, but unconfirmed).
- Store commission/external-payment rule changes (post Epic v. Apple) affecting subscription pricing were not researched.
